## Platform

- x86-64, UEFI (OVMF in QEMU), 1, 4 and 16 CPUs (4 in most groups, 1 in `x86: one CPU`, 16 in `x86: 16 CPUs`; the kernel takes up to 255) with the local APIC in xAPIC mode, and in x2APIC mode in the group `x86: x2APIC` (a test kernel switches the boot CPU as firmware would; `normal`, `busy`, `smp`, `isolation`; 211-PRT-0002), no SMT siblings in the tested configuration (`-smp N,cores=N,threads=1`).
- QEMU `pc` machine with IDE, optionally AHCI, an NVMe boot disk (group `x86: NVMe boot disk`), xHCI with USB mass storage, AC97 or Intel HD Audio (`hda` suite); physical machines boot from the USB image but are not part of the evidence.
- **No IOMMU is used.** Every device that can do DMA can read and write all physical memory.
- One node. Up to two VirtIO network cards (QEMU `virtio-net-pci`; one driver instance and one interface per card, issue 105) and an IPv4 stack (`netstack`, DHCP, ICMP, DNS, UDP, TCP) run in ring 3. Flows follow the policy broker (`netpolicy`, `netpolicy.txt` on the boot disk, issue 102): an application reaches only the destinations, term and volume its grant names; the shell's client has the operator's badge and reaches any destination. TLS 1.3 client sessions (`tls`, issue 103) run over a flow the client lends, with server certificates verified against `tlsroots.pem` on the boot disk; random numbers come from RDRAND only (no RDRAND: no TLS and no device key). Network confidentiality and integrity are claimed only for those sessions, not for the stack's other traffic. Network performance and the offload decision: [network.md](network.md).

| MC-3.4–3.6 copy/move/attenuate/revoke | partial | Copy and move are distinct; `CAP_MINT` attenuates endpoint rights and port/memory ranges and can badge an endpoint child once, only from a capability with the badge right `CAP_BADGE`, which a badged child never has (000-KRN-0073; the server tells clients' rights apart by the badge: block writes need the write badge, Appendix B.6); `CAP_REVOKE` removes all descendants (including one in a blocked send) before it returns. Not done: a step bounded in time rather than in nodes; measurements on a machine. |

| Article 4 storage | partial | A block store, `blockstore` ([docs/storage](../storage/README.md)), runs at boot over a VirtIO disk of its own where the machine has one that is blank or already holds the store (300-KRN-0025, tested in QEMU), else over a RAM disk of its own (`ramdisk#1`). Blocks are immutable and named by CIDv1, which names the format's version, the content type and the hash algorithm; unknown formats are refused (MC-4.2, 4.13). The log is append-only: nothing stored is overwritten. Every block read is checked against its CID (MC-4.8). A scrub reads every record and checks it as a mount does, between requests (a step of about 256 sectors, a pass a day) or whole on request; it logs each record that does not open or is not whole, writes nothing and repairs nothing (309-STO-0025, MC-4.8, 5.1). Every record is sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 (since layout 3, 308-STO-0021; the layout is 4): nothing of a block, a CID or a name is on the medium in the clear, a changed or moved record does not open and is counted corrupt, and a key that does not open the superblock's check is refused at mount (`key`). The volume key of a disk of its own is kept by `keystore` as the store's secret, under a key derived from the device key, and never written to the medium (308-STO-0022, MC-11.9); a RAM disk's key, and one `keystore` does not keep, is in the superblock in the clear. Memory domains (308-STO-0023, MC-4.8): a domain's names, pins and blocks are sealed under a random key of its own, kept in a record sealed under the volume key; forgetting a domain writes a tombstone and erases that record, so its records never open again, even with the volume key, and the next collection frees them. Directories are objects too (311-STO-0027): a canonical node of sorted entries, each checked against what it names; the store takes them, walks them for completeness, quotas and collection, and bounds every walk by its medium's size. Its terms are ASCII only, and it is bounded by a work area (`blocks`: 4096 terms, 32768 postings). `storefs` (311-STO-0032), `vfs.wit` over the store's names `fs/<volume>`, is host-tested and builds; it is not on the images and nothing starts it yet (311-KRN-0190). Reading, storing and publishing are separate rights by badge (MC-4.7, 4.11). Retention policy (MC-4.5, 4.11, B.3): a name keeps its newest 4 versions, each record linking the root before it, and each kept version retains its object; removing a name writes a version without a root, which ends what the name retains, and its data goes only when nothing else retains it (MC-4.8); a pin retains its object until its owner (the client's badge) ends it; what an owner's names and pins retain is charged to it, each root once, and bounded by a quota (three quarters of the medium); a block nothing retains is kept for a 60 s lease after its last put or a mount, for writes in progress. | MC-6.4–6.9 supervision | partial | `init` is the lifecycle owner of every service: the kernel sends it exit notices (`TASK_WATCH`), it restarts a failed service at most 3 times in 60 s and then quarantines it until an explicit `RUN`. Sends queued for a dead server fail with `ERR_PEER` instead of reaching the next instance; clients tell instances apart by the server PID in replies. Without `init` the system halts. Evidence: `services` suite (restart, budget, quarantine, halt), `isolation` case `f` (queued send fenced). Before a driver restarts, its device is stopped (`DEVICE_STATE`) and its DMA region cleared (MC-6.3, `ahci` and `net` suites). The block client re-attaches to a new driver instance and repeats only idempotent reads (MC-6.6). The window broker, a service, allocates application windows' surfaces and can draw on the reserve within its own quota; the reserve is free memory, not a contiguous block. The storage service is outside the boot path (MC-6.8, B.4): everything the system needs to start or to restart `blockstore` is on the boot volume, and nothing starts from the store. A restore checks the contract, schema, epoch and roots; rights come from the instance's current grants, never from the checkpoint (MC-6.11); effects begun and not recorded come back pending for reconciliation (B.4). Formats 1 and 2 are refused by their numbers. Between components (313-STO-0030) the receiver reads the sender's last checkpoint without writing it and applies what is owed to it once, the sender as its client; the sender clears what the receiver's checkpoint shows applied. ## Memory transfers (Appendix B.2)

Every memory capability a service hands to another task, and its mode (MC-2.6). `MEM_SHARE` makes a capability for a block of the caller's own heap; the modes below say what the receiver gets and when its access ends.

| Transfer | Mode | Receiver's access | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| MIND IDL buffer calls (`vfs`, `tts`, `loader`, `init`: `wire::call_buffer`) | LEASE, read-write | the server copies the request into private memory before decoding and writes the reply | `CAP_REVOKE` by the client before it reads the reply |
| `audio.play` (`mind::audio`) | LEASE, read-only | `audio_gw` copies the PCM into its DMA ring | `CAP_REVOKE` after the reply |
| `audio.record-read` | LEASE, read-write | `audio_gw` writes captured samples | `CAP_REVOKE` before the client reads them |

**What a revoke leaves** (ABI 17, 000-KRN-0152):
- `CAP_REVOKE` puts one zeroed page of the borrower's own, charged to it, at every page it mapped from the lease.
- A late write lands there and goes nowhere; a late read gives zeros, never the owner's later data.
- The page goes when the borrower frees the range.
- A borrower with no room in its quota, a device mapping and an ended task lose the mapping instead, and the next access faults.
- Evidence: the `isolation` cases `v`, `l` and `x`; the host test `a_revoked_lease_leaves_a_zeroed_page_of_the_borrowers_own`.

`SHARE_RW` is used only by the four adapters above, and between a program and its own workers. Not claimed: the end of a lease bounds the receiver's access through its mappings, not what it copied while it held them.
