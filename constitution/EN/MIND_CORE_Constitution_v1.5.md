Preamble
MIND CORE is a computational substrate for an autonomous Mind and the systems it controls. Its purpose is to provide verifiable boundaries of authority, preservation of state, controlled use of resources, and continuity of critical functions under failures within an explicitly established model.
Article 1. Minimal trusted kernel
1.1. The privileged kernel MUST contain the minimal set of mechanisms necessary to enforce the boundaries of memory, authority, execution and hardware access. Admitting a mechanism into the kernel requires a justification of its need for privilege and an assessment of the change to the trusted computing base. 1.2. The kernel's responsibilities include providing protected execution contexts, checking operations on kernel objects, authorized management of mappings, enforcement of execution budgets, basic inter-domain communication, and delivery of exceptions, failure notifications and hardware events. The exact set of mechanisms is defined by the platform profile. 1.3. Device drivers, protocol stacks, storage, interface services, application runtimes and recovery policies MUST execute outside the privileged kernel. The timer, interrupt controller, memory protection and hardware isolation mechanisms required by the kernel are identified and accounted for separately. 1.4. The policy for choosing resource recipients is separated from the mechanism that ensures their allocation is permissible. The kernel MUST prevent unauthorized creation of mappings, objects and capabilities regardless of the correctness of the user-space resource manager. 1.5. Access to a device includes separate accounting of registers, configuration, interrupts and DMA. Before DMA is enabled, the boundary on device access to memory declared by the profile MUST be enforced. If the hardware platform does not provide such a boundary, the device and its controlling code are included in the TCB of the corresponding guarantee; isolation from their incorrect or malicious behavior is not claimed. Such a profile cannot be used where precisely this isolation is required. 1.6. Unprivileged mode does not mean absence of power. The blast radius of a service is determined by all capabilities, dependencies and hardware facilities available to it. Kernel size does not substitute for analysis of the entire trusted computing base. 1.7. Creation, allocation and retyping of a protected object MUST have verifiable admissibility conditions and resource accounting. Failure, interruption or partial execution of an operation must not lead to double allocation of an exclusive resource, unaccounted objects or quota bypass. Delegating the allocation policy to user space does not grant it the right to bypass these conditions. 1.8. Reuse of memory, an execution context or a device resource MUST exclude incompatible prior accesses, use of stale references and unauthorized disclosure of residual state. Scrubbing, reinitialization or another mechanism is chosen according to the resource type and the protection model. Authorized transfer of the previous contents is a separate operation and does not require their destruction.
Article 2. Isolated state and contract messages
2.1. Each protection domain MUST have isolated state and an explicit set of capabilities. Implicit cross-domain pointers and implicitly shared mutable memory are PROHIBITED. Shared memory is permitted only through specifically granted capabilities and a defined access protocol. 2.2. An actor and a protection domain are different concepts. Actors sharing a trust boundary may be placed in the same domain. Isolation between them is claimed only to the extent provided by their runtime. For components that do not trust one another, the profile MUST specify a mechanism that preserves their boundary under the adopted attack model. 2.3. Native inter-domain communication MUST use interfaces with explicit types and versions. The contract defines the format and limits of data, permissible transferred capabilities, errors, message ordering, termination, cancellation, overflow, and the consequences of failure of the parties. 2.4. The kernel checks capabilities, validity of descriptors, envelope boundaries and resource limits. Application schemas and the meaning of messages are checked in isolated adapters of the recipient. It is PROHIBITED to consider a message trusted merely on the basis of its type identifier or the sender's promise to use a safe language. 2.5. Byte arrays and streams are permitted as explicit types. A stream may be long-lived or unbounded in the number of elements, but its buffers and resource consumption MUST be bounded. The contract MUST define backpressure, termination and cancellation. Queues with unbounded accumulation are PROHIBITED. 2.6. Memory transfer MUST distinguish data copying (COPY), transfer of exclusive ownership (MOVE), shared immutable reading (SHARE_RO) and bounded temporary granting of access (LEASE). Immutable shared reading requires the absence of permitted writes to the given version of the contents for the entire duration of the read, including writes through other mappings and DMA. A single read-only mapping on the recipient's side is not sufficient. Shared mutable access (SHARE_RW) requires a separate contract of synchronization, coherence and responsibility, and for safe profiles is strictly restricted or delegated to trusted system adapters to prevent TOCTOU attacks. 2.7. For an exclusive memory transfer, a successful operation MUST exclude further access by the previous owners, and access through derived rights issued by them, to the entire transferred object within the declared protection model. 2.8. The transfer contract MUST define the point of ownership transition, the state of the buffer in the queue, and the outcomes of cancellation and of the death of the parties. Between the moments of transfer, two owners of exclusive mutable access must not arise. 2.9. Zero-copy is permitted as an optimization provided the semantics are preserved. It is PROHIBITED to infer from the absence of copying zero cost, constant latency, or independence from the number of pages and devices. 2.10. For protocols on which critical guarantees depend, the states of the parties, permissible transitions, wait dependencies and progress conditions MUST be defined. 2.11. Validation of a message MUST remain in force until the validated values are used. For memory that an untrusted party can modify concurrently, the contract defines the method of obtaining a stable representation. 2.12. Receipt of capabilities is part of the message acceptance contract. The permissible kinds, quantity, transfer mode and conditions of acceptance of capabilities MUST be defined. 2.13. Temporary granting of access (LEASE) MUST define the permitted operations, the clock, the term, renewal, and the moment access actually ceases.
Article 3. Explicit capabilities and their lifecycle
3.1. Access to a protected object MUST rely on an explicitly granted capability. 3.2. A local capability MUST be protected from forgery and amplification by a trusted mechanism. 3.3. Creating a new domain does not give it implicit access to the creator's capabilities. Initial resources and channels are passed explicitly. 3.4. The operations of transferring, copying and attenuating a capability MUST be distinguished. 3.5. For each kind of capability, the permissible operations, the delegation mechanism, the possibility of copying, the lifetime and the revocation rules MUST be defined. 3.6. Revocation MUST have a declared scope and observable completion. After its completion, new operations through the references covered by the revocation are not permitted. 3.7. A universal bypass of capability checks based on identity, process name or a special user number is PROHIBITED. 3.8. A service acting on behalf of a client MUST bind the request to the permitted scope of actions. 3.9. Compromise analysis MUST take transitively reachable capabilities into account. 3.10. Authentication, granting of rights, checking of an operation and audit MUST be distinguishable. 3.11. A manifest, an effect declaration and saved state describe the needs of a component, but are not an authorization in themselves. 3.12. Boot MUST have a verifiable boundary at which the initial distribution of capabilities is completed. 3.13. Granting, delegation and replenishment of a resource budget MUST be authorized and subject to accounting.
Article 4. Immutable data and explicit state
4.1. Persistent storage MUST separate the identity of an immutable representation from the logical name, access rights and physical placement. 4.2. Published objects and structural manifests MUST be immutable and verifiable by content. 4.3. A stable logical entity, its immutable version and the reference to the current version MUST be distinguished. 4.4. Publication of a root MUST occur after the declared level of durability has been reached for all data necessary to restore that state. 4.5. Version history MUST have explicit links and a retention policy. 4.6. Semantic links MUST contain a type, provenance and an interpretation version. 4.7. Integrity, provenance, confidentiality, availability and durability MUST be considered separately. 4.8. The storage policy MUST define corruption detection, recovery, independence of copies and media exhaustion. 4.9. Immutability of persistent objects does not prohibit local mutable working state, caches and temporary buffers. 4.10. Publication of a change to several roots MUST declare the boundary of atomicity, isolation and durability. 4.11. The right to access an object and the obligation to retain an object MUST be distinguished. 4.12. Content identity, semantic assertions, placement, replication state and valid capabilities MUST be distinguished in the data model. 4.13. A change of hash algorithm or encoding MUST have a migration protocol for objects, references, roots and retention.
Article 5. Time and resources
5.1. Processor time, memory, kernel objects, capabilities, queues, disk space, I/O and interrupt-handling work MUST have an accounting owner, a limit or an explicitly reserved budget. 5.2. Admission of load MUST take available resources into account. 5.3. For critical functions, deadlines, permissible jitter, maximum pauses and the response to their violation MUST be specified. 5.4. Non-preemptible sections capable of delaying a critical function MUST have a justified upper bound. 5.5. Reserves for critical functions and their recovery MUST be preserved under overload of non-critical components. 5.6. Durations, deadlines and the ordering of events MUST rely on explicitly defined clocks. 5.7. If a profile supports modes of differing criticality, it MUST define the conditions of transition between them, the fate of displaced work, the reserves preserved and the conditions of return.
Article 6. Failure containment and recovery
6.1. For a component, the failure boundary, dependent functions, source of observation and permissible recovery outcomes MUST be defined. 6.2. The kernel provides containment of those violations it is able to detect and delivers a notification to the designated handler. 6.3. Recovery MUST reconcile the state of the process, its capabilities, queues, devices and published data. Before DMA memory is reused, the necessary operations of stopping access, invalidating translations and scrubbing must be completed. 6.4. A new instance of a component MUST be distinguished from the old one by a generation or an equivalent verifiable mechanism. 6.5. Repeated restarts MUST have a budget. 6.6. Acknowledgement of message delivery does not mean completion of the action. 6.7. It is PROHIBITED to claim automatic detection of any compromise. 6.8. Before a component is launched, the party responsible for its lifecycle, the recipient of failure notifications and the escalation path MUST be assigned. 6.9. Spawned work MUST have an owner of its lifecycle and resource accounting. 6.10. For a component that claims to save and restore state, there MUST exist a versioned checkpoint contract: the composition of the state, the consistency boundary, dependencies, the permissible loss of changes and the procedure for verifying restoration. The contract reconciles the saved state with accepted messages, pending replies and external effects, including effects with an unknown outcome. The required code, schemas and data are retained for the period of promised recoverability. The kernel or runtime MUST provide a safe mechanism for reconciling queue state (in-flight receipts) at the moment a checkpoint is formed, without violating isolation boundaries. Persistence is not assumed automatically for every actor. 6.11. A checkpoint MUST distinguish data, references to resources and capabilities. Memory addresses, local handles and device state are not considered a portable access authorization. 6.12. The logical identity of a service, the generation of its instance and its place of execution MUST be distinguished.
