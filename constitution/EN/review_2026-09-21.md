# Review of MIND CORE and Marain — September 21, 2026

**Conclusion:** Constitution v1.4 and RFC Marain v0.2 are a substantive foundation for design and a prototype. Constitution v1.5 and RFC v0.3 in their current form are less suited to the role of replacement specifications: the condensation removed essential conditions, and the new `reconcile` guarantee is incorrect. I recommend building the next full revision on v1.4/v0.2, carrying over into it only the refined additions.

This is a review of requirements and design decisions. The implementation, its performance and proofs were not examined here. External sources were studied on 21.09.2026; the recommendations for MIND CORE below are engineering conclusions, not guarantees automatically inherited from other projects.

## 1. Assessment of the four documents

| Document | Assessment | Recommended role |
|---|---|---|
| [Constitution v1.4](MIND_CORE_Constitution_v1.4.md) | Separates invariants, mechanisms, profiles and evidence well. Needs a cleanup of categorical rationales and more convenient requirement tracing. | Basis of the next normative revision |
| [Constitution v1.5](MIND_CORE_Constitution_v1.5.md) | Article 1 is largely preserved; Articles 2–6 are substantially cut. Articles 7–12 are explicitly inherited from v1.4. The status of the appendices has changed. | Draft of amendments; not yet usable as a full replacement |
| [Marain v0.2](RFC_001_Marain_v0.2.md) | A strong architectural RFC: authorization, ownership, unknown outcomes, ABI, recovery and acceptance are tied together. A formal language specification is still to come. | Research RFC and basis for a prototype |
| [Marain v0.3](RFC_001_Marain_v0.3.md) | Useful intentions, but most of the semantics has been replaced by enumerations. `reconcile` promises an impermissible restoration of ownership on timeout. | A brief overview after corrections; not a "final specification" |

Mechanical comparison of the Constitution's paragraphs: of the 114 paragraphs of v1.4, 66 are restated in v1.5; 51 of them are shorter, 13 coincide after removal of Markdown backticks, 2 are expanded — 2.6 and 6.10. Another 48 paragraphs of Articles 7–12 are preserved **by explicit reference**, so calling them lost would be incorrect. The number of reductions by itself does not measure the damage; significant examples are given below.

## 2. Blocking error: reconcile cannot return authority on timeout

**Location:** [RFC v0.3, §7.5, line 20](RFC_001_Marain_v0.3.md). Compare with [v0.2, §7.3 and §7.5, lines 180–206](RFC_001_Marain_v0.2.md).

The new version promises: if a timeout or partition is confirmed, the exclusive ownership from escrow will be returned to the sender. What is confirmed here is the absence of connectivity, not the absence of acceptance, execution or continuing access on the other side.

Counterexample:

1. The gateway passed the recipient the right to use a resource.
2. The recipient began the action; the reply was lost.
3. The sender registered a timeout and got the right back.
4. The recipient continues the action, or its delayed command arrives later. Exclusive control is violated.

If escrow has not yet released the right to the recipient and can reliably establish this from its own state, return is possible. If the resource is guaranteed to reject the old epoch, a new grant is possible after fencing according to a defined protocol. The words "trusted gateway" by themselves do not create these conditions. Control over future commands also does not undo an already performed effect and does not return lost data.

In Chubby, late requests are restricted by a sequencer checked by the **receiving server**; a delay before re-granting is described as an imperfect substitute. This directly supports the need for fencing at the executor. [Burrows, Chubby, §2.4](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/osdi06/tech/full_papers/burrows/burrows_html/).

**Proposed replacement for the norm:**

> `reconcile` performs an authorized reconciliation of the operation outcome and the ownership state by operation ID. Timeout and loss of connectivity do not by themselves permit the return of exclusive ownership. Return is permitted upon confirmed non-acceptance of the transfer, or after a protocol that excludes prior access and determines the fate of accepted commands. If the evidence is insufficient, `OutcomeUnknown` is retained; the resource remains accounted for. The conditions for completing reconciliation, its budget and the response to prolonged unavailability are defined by the profile.

Two results need to be distinguished: **the outcome of the action** and **the current owner of the resource**. One can already safely change the owner without yet knowing the result of the previous command. `reconcile`, resource return, retry and compensation are different operations with different capabilities. The quota of unresolved records must limit the admission of new operations; when it is exhausted, old obligations cannot simply be forgotten.

In addition, the phrase in v0.3 §7.3 "the transport has exactly two outcomes" has lost the restriction to **local `try_send`**. For a remote operation, the observable result may remain unknown. Restore the exact scope from v0.2.

## 3. What was lost in condensing the Constitution

Below we discuss the disappearance of an explicit norm from the restated paragraphs. Some general principles can still be derived from other articles, but this is not the same as preserving the exact obligation and the verification criterion.

| Paragraphs v1.4 → v1.5 | What disappeared or weakened | Why restore it |
|---|---|---|
| 2.7–2.8 | CPU/DMA and re-creation of mappings; failure when MOVE is impossible; protection of an adjacent range; resource accounting after a crash | Exclusivity must be ensured on all access paths, including errors |
| 2.10 | Checking the composition of protocols, queues, budgets, failures and progress assumptions | A typed channel does not prove the absence of deadlocks in the whole system |
| 2.11–2.12 | Prohibition of re-trusting changed lengths/descriptors; parsing budgets; fate of non-accepted capabilities | A specific protection against double-fetch and resource attacks is removed |
| 2.13 | Lease expiry does not prove cessation of CPU/DMA access; completion and scrubbing are required | A timer is not a mechanism for cutting off access |
| 3.4–3.6 | A derived right is no broader than the original; fate of operations already started; protection against resurrecting an old handle; limits of revocation | Verifiable attenuation and revocation are needed, not just their names |
| 3.7–3.12 | Separation of initial/recovery authority; conditions for removing bootstrap authority; details of confused deputy and current authorization | Retained broad authority and intermediaries are part of the real TCB |
| 3.13; 5.1 | A copy of a handle does not create a budget; an intermediary's work has a payer | Otherwise client quotas are bypassed through a service |
| 4.2–4.5 | Encoding and algorithm of the CID; concurrent update of Head; partial write; GC roots and unlinked nodes | CAS by itself does not provide commit, retention and consistency |
| 4.6–4.8; 4.11–4.12 | Model inference as a source assertion; access checks in search; deduplication leaks; paid retention | Especially significant for the memory of a cognitive system |
| 5.2–5.6 | Outcome of overload; shared hardware in timing analysis; priority inversion; background work; distinction of clocks | Limits without these conditions do not provide the claimed temporal isolation |
| 6.4–6.9 | Handling of stale replies; independent supervisor reserve; idempotence and unknown outcome; failure of the recoverer itself; fate of descendants | A restart must have defined consequences in all states |
| 6.11–6.12 | Restore does not return revoked rights; a snapshot does not clone exclusive authority; fencing and in-flight during migration | Rolling back state must not roll back security |

In v1.4 these norms are in Articles 2–6, lines 85–197; in v1.5 the corresponding reductions are lines 10–18. The most dangerous way to condense such a document is to keep the first sentence of a paragraph and delete the failure conditions and exceptions: it is precisely they that determine the behavior of the system in difficult cases.

## 4. What was lost in Marain v0.3

There are references to the old example (§12) and alternatives (§14). There is no general rule "all the remaining text of v0.2 applies unchanged". Therefore the short sections cannot confidently be read as a complete successor specification.

| Sections of v0.2 | What needs to be restored |
|---|---|
| §§1–2 | Boundaries of promises, threat model, distribution of guarantees between compiler/runtime/kernel/service, design status of the examples |
| §3 | Left-to-right order, overflow, trap versus Result, UTF-8, closure effects, monomorphization of exports, limits of purity |
| §§4–5 | Handler effect-row rules; zero resume and cleanup; prohibition of escaping continuation/borrow; explicit spawn bindings; no Cap inside a copyable val; drop is not revoke |
| §6 | ARC cascades, bounded cleanup queue, accounting of host calls, forced interruption; `rt` outside the MVP |
| §7 | The Uncertain automaton, receipt versus effect, return of payload on Rejected, atomicity of enqueue/MOVE, exclusion of remote MOVE from the initial profile, limits of session types |
| §8 | Version of the semantics and of the canonical codec; composition of CodeID/BuildKey; recursive groups; test vectors; retention; AOT verification at launch |
| §§9–10 | Inbox/outbox and unresolved effects in the checkpoint; rebind with current authorization; prohibition of repeated real effects during replay; full inventory of nondeterminism |
| §11 | Versioned ABI profile; absence of implicit WASI; limits already at lifting; companion behavior contract; FFI boundary |
| §§15–17 | Concrete acceptance criteria, negative scenarios, open questions and the map of conformance to the Constitution |

The consequence is that different implementations can honestly follow v0.3 and diverge on overflow, cancellation, action retries, resource cleanup and code identity.

## 5. New additions: what to accept, what to refine

### SHARE_RW: the restriction is correct, the novelty is overstated

The prohibition in the safe Core MVP **already exists** in [v0.2 §5, line 125](RFC_001_Marain_v0.2.md). It is worth keeping. Placing dangerous access in an adapter with narrow rights is useful, but the adapter being trusted does not eliminate modification of the buffer by an attacker.

**Require from the adapter:** a bounded private copy, with validation of exactly that copy; or enforced immutability for the duration of use; or a separately justified concurrent algorithm. A mutex helps only against participants who follow the protocol. A copy of a concurrently mutable structure may itself be inconsistent: it still has to be validated, including the relationships between fields. [SafeFetch, USENIX Security 2024](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/duta).

In the Constitution, keep invariant 2.11. Describe the specific Core prohibition and the admission of adapters in the profile, enumerating who may use which modes.

### Checkpoint receipts: a useful obligation, an insufficient specification

The addition in [v1.5 6.10, line 18](MIND_CORE_Constitution_v1.5.md) makes infrastructure support for checkpoints more explicit. But "reconciling queues at the moment of the snapshot" does not define a consistent point between the actor's state, the queue, the published Head and the external executor.

Proposed norm: the persistent execution profile defines a consistent checkpoint protocol for state, queues and unresolved effects; the responsible components provide the necessary bounded operations. The mechanism — quiescence, a journal, barriers or another — is described separately. This norm should not be used to put domain-specific checkpoint logic into the kernel.

A practical example: Flink separately distinguishes the consistency of its own state and the result in an external system; end-to-end exactly-once requires the participation of the external recipient. This protocol cannot be automatically transferred to a physical actuator. [Apache Flink, exactly-once](https://flink.apache.org/2018/02/28/an-overview-of-end-to-end-exactly-once-processing-in-apache-flink-with-apache-kafka-too/).

### Handle checks: keep, extend the exact contract

The check of type, owner and generation **was already** in [v0.2 §5, line 119](RFC_001_Marain_v0.2.md), and of current authorization in §4.3. The new wording of §11 of v0.3 is useful as an explicit requirement for all relevant ABI transitions.

However, three fields do not replace checking the permitted operation, scope, revocation state, protocol and quota. Checking and application must remain consistent under concurrent revocation. A stale handle needs an explicit slot-reuse protocol; the word "epoch" without a way of binding it to the request does not solve ABA.

Numeric indices must not be a secret. They select rights in the protected context of the calling domain. Guessing the index of a right already granted to this domain is not a privilege escalation; access to another domain's context must be impossible. For mutually untrusted actors within a single runtime, the real separation mechanism must be named, rather than relying on hiding a number. [seL4: CSpace and invocation](https://docs.sel4.systems/Tutorials/capabilities.html), [WebAssembly: security model boundaries](https://webassembly.org/docs/security/).

## 6. Normativity and editorial discipline

**Conflict over the status of appendices.** [v1.4, lines 25–29](MIND_CORE_Constitution_v1.4.md) makes the articles normative and the appendices explanations/proposals. [v1.5, line 4](MIND_CORE_Constitution_v1.5.md) declares the engineering profile normative and lists B, C, D, E. Meanwhile, the imported B permits alternatives, D contains a plan, and E contains historical SHA-256 values. This is a substantive change to the conformance rules, not formatting.

**Numbering is mixed up.** v1.5 Appendix D refers to "phases M0–M7" of v1.4. Constitution v1.4 has stages **0, I–VII**; M0–M7 belong to the language RFC. The dependencies are easy to misread.

**The "archive" is not defined.** v1.5 speaks of moving Appendices A/F/G to an archive, but no path is given. In the reviewed set they are available within v1.4; there is no separately designated archive. Ordinary working links and a fixed source version are needed. The document precedence policy and the glossary from v1.4 have also disappeared.

**What to do:**

1. Publish the full normative revision and a separate brief summary. If only an amendment is issued, explicitly state the base revision, the exact paragraphs being replaced and the inheritance rule.
2. Preserve the hierarchy: Constitution invariants → architectural contracts → named platform/language profiles → roadmap and rationales.
3. Assign stable IDs to requirements; for each, link the mechanism, responsible layer, assumptions and evidence. Acceptance tables already exist — they need to be traced, not duplicated.
4. Restore the definitions of MUST/MUST NOT/MAY and precedence in case of conflict. RFC 8174 is useful as a model of explicit normative language; claiming IETF conformance for Russian text is not required. [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8174/).
5. Remove "final specification" from v0.3: the grammar, codec and runtime profile from the open questions of v0.2 are not yet defined in the new text.

## 7. Best practices for the system

| Decision | How to apply in MIND CORE | What to avoid | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimal kernel and explicit TCB | For each guarantee, enumerate the kernel, boot, firmware, runtime, drivers, DMA and hardware assumptions | "A microkernel automatically makes the whole OS secure" | [seL4: assumptions](https://sel4.systems/Verification/assumptions.html) |
| Capability authority | Narrow rights; attenuation; controlled delegation; separate boot/recovery capabilities; checking at the executor | A global lookup service with implicit authority; broad parser capabilities; secrecy of the index instead of protecting the table | [seL4 capabilities](https://docs.sel4.systems/Tutorials/capabilities.html) |
| Full accounting of work | Bound CPU/memory/queues, lifting, host calls, cleanup, compile and recovery; assign a payer to the intermediary | Client consumption turns into unbounded service work | [seL4 MCS](https://docs.sel4.systems/Tutorials/mcs), [Wasmtime Config](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/api/wasmtime/struct.Config.html#method.epoch_interruption) |
| Failure protocols | Operation ID; explicit commit points; unknown outcome; fencing at the resource; defined fate of old commands | Blind retry after timeout; "the restart cancelled the action" | [Chubby](https://www.usenix.org/legacy/event/osdi06/tech/full_papers/burrows/burrows_html/) |
| State and recovery | Reconcile durable state, inbox/outbox, unresolved effects, retention and rebind | A memory snapshot or a queue snapshot as a sufficient recovery protocol | [Flink](https://flink.apache.org/2018/02/28/an-overview-of-end-to-end-exactly-once-processing-in-apache-flink-with-apache-kafka-too/) |
| Secure update | Separate key roles; check versions and expiry; define offline policy, recovery and state migration | A single signature plus A/B as full protection against rollback/freeze | [TUF specification](https://theupdateframework.github.io/specification/latest/) |
| Early protocol verification | Model revoke/MOVE/checkpoint/fencing before optimization; then link the model to fault tests of the implementation | Taking a successful test bench as proof of correctness of all executions | [AWS: How Amazon Web Services Uses Formal Methods](https://assets.amazon.science/67/f9/92733d574c11ba1a11bd08bfb8ae/how-amazon-web-services-uses-formal-methods.pdf) |

This supports the direction of v1.4, not the need to rewrite everything from scratch. For a first slice, one platform, COPY IPC, one Head service and one recoverable actor are reasonable. Zero-copy, cross-node exclusive ownership and a qualified `rt` require profiles of their own.

## 8. Best practices for the language

**Keep the Core small.** ADTs, Result, explicit effects, affine resources, one-shot continuations, local automata and bounded actors form a coherent set. But each mechanism must have rules for combined use. Work on linearity and effect handlers shows real errors when they are naively combined; the prohibition of multi-shot in v0.2 is a reasonable simplification. [Authors of Soundly Handling Linearity, POPL 2024](https://blog.sigplan.org/2024/08/12/soundly-handling-linearity/).

**Effects do not replace authorization.** Keep the separation of effect / handler / capability. `Net` indicates the kind of action; the recipient, term, scope and right to delegate remain properties of the specific capability. A complete binding plan does not promise successful execution after revocation.

**ARC is a memory strategy.** Perceus provides concrete results on reference counting, reuse and garbage-freedom within its model. For MIND CORE this is a basis for investigating the strategy, not a proof of hard real-time. Restore from v0.2 the limits on the deallocation cascade and the quotas for deferred cleanup. [Perceus, primary publication](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/perceus-garbage-free-reference-counting-with-reuse/).

**WIT is an interface layer.** WIT defines interfaces and types, including resource ownership/borrow; it does not specify protocol behavior. The MIND companion contract must separately define errors, ordering, limits, cancellation and rights. [WIT Reference](https://component-model.bytecodealliance.org/design/wit.html).

**Wasm metering does not cover all execution.** The Wasmtime documentation explicitly notes: fuel and epochs do not interrupt blocking inside a host call. Hence limits and cancellation are needed on the host side, plus accounting of its CPU/memory. This is one more argument for restoring the detailed §6.2 of v0.2. [Wasmtime: blocking host calls](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/api/wasmtime/struct.Config.html#method.epoch_interruption).

**Definition hashes are useful given precise semantics.** Unison confirms the viability of the idea of identifying a definition by hash, with names as separate metadata. For Marain, keep the distinctions between SourceCID/CodeID/BuildKey/ArtifactCID and independent test vectors. Equality of identity does not replace checking the compatibility of the protocol, bindings and state schema. [Unison: The big idea](https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/the-big-idea/).

**A new language must be compared against an alternative.** Recommendation: run the same scenario on research Marain and on an existing language with generated MIND bindings. Compare diagnosable errors, the amount of trusted code, memory, IPC cost, debugging and maintenance. Begin the comparison at M0; use the M6 pilot for the final decision. Rust with bindings is a reasonable baseline for this project, but not an automatic replacement for all of Marain's effect/protocol capabilities.

## 9. What else to refine even in v1.4/v0.2

These issues did not arise from the condensation; the source documents themselves assign some of them to future stages.

1. **Formal semantics of Core.** Fix the grammar, kind/ownership rules, closure captures, polymorphic effects, handler nesting, zero resume and trap cleanup. An example of a negative requirement: a copyable value cannot hide a Cap inside a closure or a parametric container. A reference evaluator and comparison with the Wasm backend are needed, already planned in M0–M3.
2. **Exact execution profile.** Choose versions of the runtime/Component Model/WIT, permitted imports, lifting limits, metering and cancellation of host calls. Specifying "Wasm" is not enough. Checking the local model on a host bench does not attest hardware MOVE/DMA.
3. **One fully worked-out checkpoint protocol.** Where is the commit? Who stores the receipt? When is ACK permitted? What happens after a crash between an effect and the recording of its result? How do revoke and fencing survive restore? Fix the storage model and the point after which dedup records are forgotten; old retries after deletion must be rejected or have an explicitly limited guarantee.
4. **Reconcile the logging example with §7.5.** The WIT in §11 of v0.2 returns `result<_, log-error>`, including `unavailable`, but does not show an unknown append outcome. Either the companion contract proves failure before commit, or the API/protocol must represent an operation ID and an unknown outcome. This is an underspecification of the example, not a proven implementation error.
5. **Clarify the role of the language in the cognitive plane.** A Core without floats is suitable for orchestration, but does not describe the tensor/GPU computations themselves. For the pilot it is simpler to treat them as a separate bounded service: versioned model/inputs, budget, cancellation, provenance of the result and declared limits of replay. This is a proposed decomposition, not a commitment to extend the language immediately.
6. **Clean up the polemical rationales of v1.4.** In Appendix F.1 the theses "the attack surface equals the volume of privileged code" and "the cost of IPC has been removed by practice" are excessive; in F.3, ACLs, UID/GID and root are reduced to a single thesis about the "structural unsolvability of the confused deputy". It would be better to describe concrete ambient-authority and deputy scenarios. Norms 1.6 and 3.8–3.10 are already more precise than these rationales. Do not turn an engineering choice into a universal assessment of all operating systems.

## 10. Proposed order of corrections

| Priority | Action | Readiness criterion |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Fix reconcile and the scope of Accepted/Rejected | A timeout does not create a second owner; uncertainty is representable |
| P0 | Restore the full articles and sections of v1.4/v0.2 | Every previous requirement is preserved, explicitly replaced or removed with justification |
| P1 | Formalize SHARE_RW, handles and checkpoint as precise amendments | There is a mechanism, scope, failures and a verifiable result |
| P1 | Restore document precedence, appendix status, links and open questions | The reader can unambiguously determine the requirement in force |
| P1 | Verify the ownership/revoke/checkpoint/fencing models | Invariants, assumptions, counterexamples and corresponding verification scenarios are defined |
| P2 | Implement one vertical slice and compare the languages | Bounded actor → service call → durable state → crash → restore/rebind; costs measured |

Especially important for this slice are: a lost reply after commit; revocation between binding and the call; a gateway crash and recovery of its journal; replay without repeating the physical action; a foreign/stale handle; an oversized ABI input; a queue and cleanup at the quota limit; a checkpoint close to GC and Head publication. These are proposed checks, not results of executed tests.
